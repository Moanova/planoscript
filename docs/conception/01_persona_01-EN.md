# Planoscript :: Persona 01 - Jen Yen


##Jen Yen, video game writer
- **Description**:
    28 years old, freelance writer, with 4 years’ experience in video games and, to a lesser extent, in AI-assisted anime.
    She collaborates with small teams and other independent creators.
    Her current project is the creation of a new studio’s first game, an RPG.
    She is working with the founder and a few developers on a demo designed to convince an investor to commit to the project.
- **Challenges**:
    Due to their interactive nature, RPG narratives are non-linear and feature numerous branching paths determined by the players’ choices.
    The imaginative world of these stories also presents situations that are often more complex than those in traditional fiction: multiverses, temporal paradoxes, alternative endings, etc.
    The coherence of the plots across all their branches is therefore fundamental, and no narrative dead ends are permissible.
    It is also necessary to properly manage how the progression of the narratives is conditioned by the acquisition of artefacts or skills.
    Finally, it is essential to be able to optimise the efficiency of the workflow using tools suited to these specific characteristics.
- **Objectives**:
    1. To visually structure modular narrative frameworks in a flexible way.
    2. To clearly organise alternatives based on the players’ possible choices.
    3. To easily establish links between characters, artefacts and events.
    4. To export narrative frameworks into a universal format for sharing with the team.
- **Frustrations**:
    1. Her workflow relies on a combination of several tools (Google Docs, Twine), but neither the tools nor their combination fully meet her needs, and it is difficult to visualise all the conditional branches of the narrative without risking errors.
    2. Professional tools are beyond her budget, but also too complex and not intuitive enough for a writer (Final Draft, Arc Studio Pro). They are also over-specced for the task.
    3. Collaboration with developers is not smooth, as they require formatted files that must be created manually.
- **Constraints**:
    1. Tools: the work must focus on the actual writing and not on learning a complex technical tool.
    2. Budget: purchasing an expensive software licence is not currently feasible.
- **Typical quote**:
    "I spend all my time organising notes in Google Docs and linking them to Twine diagrams, but in the end, I still can’t make sense of it all.
    I need a specialised tool that would allow me to build all the sequences directly and annotate them, without having to write a single line of code."
- **Technical skills**:
    Standard office software and a smattering of Twine, but no coding skills.
    The ideal tool would be an intuitive drag-and-drop utility running on Windows and macOS.
